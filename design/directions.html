Calendar app · design exploration · 28 July 2026

Three ways to draw a day that is mostly open.

Every direction below renders the same Tuesday with the same tasks, the same six hours and twenty five minutes of open time, and the same two tasks that decide whether the day was good. Only the visual language changes.

Each one takes one of the three directions in section 11 of the spec and commits to it rather than blending. The grid is drawn at true scale, so a thirty minute obligation really is a third the height of a ninety minute open block.

What to look for

Whether open time actually wins your eye before anything else does. That is the whole product.

What is fixed

Layout, hierarchy, and the data. Those come from the spec and do not change between the three.

What is not decided

Fonts, colour, and texture are all still moveable inside whichever direction you pick.

Direction one

Ballast

Obligations are small, dense, dark objects that sit low and cast a shadow. Open time is a lit field that floats above them. A heavy day looks bottom loaded. An open day looks buoyant.

Palette

Marine ink, sea glass for open time, apricot reserved for today's two.

Type

Bricolage Grotesque for the figure and all labels
Newsreader for task names and body

The risk

Dark ground. Sea glass is doing a lot of work and has to stay legible at 10pm without going neon.

Note the physics: every scheduled block has a bottom edge and a shadow underneath it, so it reads as resting on something. Open blocks have a glow above them instead and no bottom edge at all.

Direction two

Terrain

Time as ground. Scheduled work is built structure with hard edges and a cast shadow. Open time is unbuilt land, contour hatched, shown at true scale. A week reads like a survey map with a lot of undeveloped acreage.

Palette

Lichen ground, surveyor's blue for open land, rust for outside obligations only.

Type

Anybody stretched wide for the figure
Archivo for everything else

The risk

The hatching can get busy on a full week. It may need to thin out as the grid gets smaller.

This is the only direction that reads well printed, and the only one where the month view is genuinely beautiful as pure texture. It is also the most opinionated, so it will either land immediately or not at all.

Direction three

Daylight

The grid is lit by the hour. Dawn at the top, noon in the middle, dusk at the bottom. Scheduled blocks are silhouettes cut into the light. Open slots are simply where nothing is blocking the sun.

Palette

Indigo night, gold noon, rose for the current time, sky for weekly totals.

Type

Kalnia for the figure and today's two
Instrument Sans for the grid and body
Kalnia draws a flat topped 4 that reads as a 1 under about 26px, so every small number falls to Instrument Sans.

The risk

Text sits on a moving background. Every block needs a contrast check at both the bright and dark ends.

The strongest emotional read of the three, and it needs no explanation. It is also the most fragile: the gradient has to shift with her real wake and sleep times or the metaphor breaks, and a 10pm block sitting on near black needs a different text treatment from a 12pm one.

Picking one

My read, if it helps:

  • Ballast is the safest and the most honest to the thesis. Weight is the most direct way to say "this costs you something and this does not."
  • Terrain is the funkiest and scales best to the week and month views, where the other two get quieter.
  • Daylight is the one you would show someone. It is also the one with the most edge cases in the grid.

Say a number and I will build phase two against it. Blending them is the one thing I would push back on, since each of these gets its meaning from being the only rule on the page.
